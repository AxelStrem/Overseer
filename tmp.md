Drum Trainer (DrumTrainer) is a small Godot app for practising on electronic drums. It listens to MIDI inputs: the kit being played, a ground-truth track to play along with, and a look-ahead channel that shows what is coming. It matches each hit against the track, scores the performance on several metrics - points with a combo bonus, notes hit, average points, performance, RMS timing error - draws it all on a 3D board, and writes each finished exercise into the practice Overseer document via Overseer API on HTTP.  There is no release: the owner runs it from the editor.\n\n DrumTrainer opens every MIDI input (`OS.open_midi_inputs()`), and the settings display picks three of them:\n\n- the **kit** the owner plays (`user_input`);\n- the **ground truth** (`gt_input`) - the track to play along with, sent by another program as MIDI;\n- the **look-ahead** (`ahead_input`) - the same notes sent early, drawn as what is coming.\n\nEach hit of the kit is paired with the ground-truth note it answers, within `max_tolerance_time`\n(0.15 s), and judged good within `good_tolerance_time` (0.03 s), early, late, weak, bad or skipped\n(`judge.gd`). The judgement drops a coloured note on the 3D board (`note_*.tscn`) and feeds the\nscore.\n\nThe metrics, per track, counted by `judge.gd` into `track.gd`: points with a combo bonus\n(`combo_bonus`), notes hit and missed, best combo, average points, performance (`push_perf`: a\nrunning and a total figure), and the RMS timing error. When a track ends, the summary display shows\nthem, and one `PracticeRecord` is written into the practice document.\n\n## Where things are\n\n- `bonk.gd`, the root node of `main.tscn`, which feeds `Judge` notes and the clock and draws what it\n  returns:\n  - the signal pitches;\n  - `_ready`, which opens the MIDI inputs;\n  - `reset_track`, `push_track`;\n  - `note_ahead_played` and the BPM sync;\n  - `note_played`: the signal pitches and the exercises, and the track's record, then the note\n    handed to `judge.add_note`;\n  - `_input`, MIDI events to the three inputs;\n  - `spawn_note3d`, `predict_note`;\n  - `_physics_process`, which draws what `judge.advance` returns, and judges a kit note left without\n    a pair by the auto tracker's confidence;\n  - the three input selectors' handlers, and the Esc menu's dialogs (`make_setting_dialogs`).\n- `judge.gd` (`class_name Judge`, no scene), the rules, run headless by the tests:\n  - the note flags, `max_tolerance_time`, `good_tolerance_time`, `alt_pitches`;\n  - the scoring (`combo_bonus`, `push_perf`, `push_score`, `track_note_hit`, `track_note_skipped`,\n    `track_note_bad`);\n  - `add_note` and `advance`, the pairing and judging of hits with notes;\n  - `should_record` and `record_fields`, what a track writes into the practice document.\n- `track.gd`: a track's figures, which `Judge` fills in.\n- `auto_tracker.gd`: the pattern and BPM prediction.\n- `record_sender.gd`: the write into the practice document.\n- `settings.gd`: the Esc menu's settings, added by a line in `DEFAULTS` and kept between launches in\n  `user://settings.cfg`.\n- `Tracks.gd`: the catalogue, exercise id (a MIDI pitch) to name.\n- `main.tscn`: the board, the displays and their labels, which `bonk.gd` reaches by `@onready` paths.\n- The 3D controls of the menus: `button.gd`, `checkbox3d.gd`, `setting_row3d.gd`,\n  `horizontal_selector.gd`.\n- The board's small scripts: `note.gd`, `floater.gd`, `combo_bar.gd`, `conveyor.gd`,\n  `track_message.gd`.\n- `tests/`: the headless tests (see \"Checking your work\").\n\n## The practice document\n\nResults go to `ap31/drums.os`, the owner's practice document, a `drum_tracker` tab with a list\n`History` of `PracticeRecord`s and a list `Exercises` keyed by `id` (the same ids as\n`Tracks.Catalog`).\n\nThe document is on the Overseer server, where the owner's other tools and the bot change it too.\n`record_sender.gd` sends each record there with `POST /v1/append` to `drum_tracker/History`, as an\naccount with a key; `API.md` in `{overseer}` describes the call. A record goes into a queue file\n(`user://pending_records.json`) before it is sent, and leaves it only once the server has it, so a\nserver out of reach loses nothing. The server's address, the document and the key file are the\nsettings `overseer_document` and `overseer_key_file`, and none of them is in the repository.\n\nIts copy in `{personal-stats}`, `documents/ap31/drums.os`, is the same document: a change there\nreaches the server with the next backup. Change it only when the item asks for it - a new exercise\nin `Exercises`, say - and never touch the records already in `History`. A change to the shape of\n`PracticeRecord` changes what the history means, as a change to a metric does (see \"Rules\").\n\nNever write to the practice document to try something: it is the owner's record of their practice.\nA check that needs a record written uses a copy, served by `overseer-server` from\n`{overseer}/src-tauri/target/release` on loopback.\n\n## Rules\n\n- **Nobody can play the drums here, or see or hear the app.** A change to how hits are judged or\n  scored is checked by reasoning, and best by a case in `tests/run_tests.gd`, which feeds note\n  events to `Judge` without MIDI hardware: say which, and what the owner should play to try it. A\n  change to the board is\n  checked by the probe for errors only; say what the owner should look at.\n- **Running the app opens every MIDI input**, and on Windows an input is usually one program's at a\n  time. Run it (`probe.ps1 run`) only when the owner's Drum Tracker is not running - if\n  `tasklist` shows a Godot process for this project, leave it and say so. `probe.ps1 check` loads\n  the project headless and opens nothing.\n- **A change to a metric changes what the history means.** The records in `drums.os` go back to\n  2025. A formula changed here makes new records incomparable with old ones: say so in the\n  comment, and propose how to mark the change (a version in the record, say) rather than doing it\n  silently.\n- **Exercise ids are MIDI pitches**, shared by `Tracks.Catalog`, the ground-truth tracks the owner\n  plays along with, and `drums.os`'s `Exercises`. Never renumber one.\n- GDScript: match the file you are in. The code is mostly untyped. New code is typed where it is\n  cheap, but an existing function is never retyped just because it is being touched.\n\n## Tools\n\nIn `{bundle}/tools`:\n\n| | |\n| --- | --- |\n| `probe.ps1` | Runs the app with the fork's binary and classifies what it logged, so the benign noise every Godot run produces does not read as failure. `check` loads the project headless and reports script parse errors, missing resources and scenes that fail - a few seconds, no window, no MIDI. `run -Frames N` launches it for N frames and reports what the scripts and the renderer logged, MIDI inputs opened. Both write the full log to the scratch folder and print its path. |\n\n## Checking your work\n\n```powershell\npowershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File {bundle}/tools/probe.ps1 check -Project \"{drum-trainer}\" -Engine \"{godot-local-development}/bin/godot.windows.editor.x86_64.console.exe\"\npowershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File {bundle}/tools/probe.ps1 run -Frames 300 -Project \"{drum-trainer}\" -Engine \"{godot-local-development}/bin/godot.windows.editor.x86_64.console.exe\"\npowershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File \"{drum-trainer}/tests/test.ps1\" -Engine \"{godot-local-development}/bin/godot.windows.editor.x86_64.console.exe\"\n```\n\n- `check` after any change to a script, scene or resource.\n- `run` after anything that happens at runtime, and only as the rules say.\n- **The exit code is 0 even when scripts failed to parse.** Read the probe's verdict, never the\n  exit code.\n- `test.ps1` runs `tests/run_tests.gd` headless, with no scene, MIDI or server, so it is safe while\n  the app is running: the pairing, the judging and the scoring, the settings and the record queue.\n  Run it after any change to those, and add a case there for new behaviour. Its verdict is its\n  last line, `TESTS PASSED n` or `TESTS FAILED k/n`, for the same reason as the probe's.\n\n## Things that have bitten before\n\n- **Every run ends in benign errors:** `Condition \"!configured\" is true`, leaked RIDs and resources\n  at exit, the Vulkan loader's registry warning. `probe.ps1` filters them; a grep for `ERROR` does\n  not.\n- `note_ahead.tscn`, `note_ahead_alter.tscn` and `note_good.tscn` are about 750 KB each: meshes\n  saved inline. Never read them whole; `grep` them.\n\n## Code\n\nWrite like the surrounding code: tabs, the names and the layout `bonk.gd` already uses. Scenes are\ntext - keep diffs small, and never reformat a `.tscn` wholesale. Documentation describes\nmechanisms, never test counts or file listings.\n\n## Complexity, for scale\n\nItems already built, and what is like them:\n- `trivial`: `rms-is-mse`, a figure in the record corrected; a new exercise added to `Tracks.Catalog`.\n- `low`: `record-nan`, tracks with no hits left out of the records; a new metric computed from what\n  `track.gd` already counts and shown on the summary display.\n- `medium`: `persistent-settings`, the settings store and inputs chosen by name; `server-records`,\n  records written through the server's API, with a queue.\n- `high`: `scoring-harness`, the judging moved into `Judge` with its tests; a change to how hits are\n  paired with notes, or to the auto tracker's prediction.\n
